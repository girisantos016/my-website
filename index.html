01/About Me

About Me

I am Santosh Giri, an Assistant Professor of Computer Engineering at the Department of Electronics and Computer Engineering, Pulchowk Campus, Institute of Engineering, Tribhuvan University, Nepal. With 10+ years in academia and 13+ years in professional experience, my work combines academic teaching, research, student supervision, academic leadership, and technology-driven institutional development.

My research interests lie in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, and Generative AI, with a particular focus on developing practical intelligent systems for real-world applications. My recent research explores automatic monument detection, digital cultural heritage preservation, 3D scene reconstruction, and AI-based wildlife detection and non-lethal deterrence.

I lead and contribute to research and academic development initiatives, including a University Grants Commission (UGC), Nepal-funded project on AI-based rhesus macaque detection and deterrence. I have also contributed to research on Nepal's cultural heritage through deep-learning-based monument detection and 3D reconstruction, including international research activities associated with SOI Asia's DHASH digital cultural heritage initiative.

Beyond research, I have supervised 40+ undergraduate engineering projects and postgraduate theses, contributed to the development and revision of 15+ academic curricula and course syllabi at IOE, and delivered over 35 invited lectures, faculty development programs, and professional training workshops. My academic leadership includes program coordination, departmental administration, curriculum development, and institutional technology planning.

I also contribute technical expertise in AI laboratory development, GPU-enabled computing infrastructure, educational technology, procurement planning, and software-based institutional transformation. Through my teaching, research, and professional collaborations, I aim to connect academic knowledge with practical engineering solutions and meaningful societal impact.

Research project 01

Deep Learning-Based Approaches for Cultural Monument Analysis and Documentation in Nepal

Deep LearningComputer VisionDigital Cultural Heritage3D Reconstruction

Project overview

Nepal's cultural monuments are an important part of its history, architecture, and cultural identity. Digital technologies offer opportunities to document these monuments, support their identification, and create digital representations for preservation, research, and public engagement.

This research initiative investigates deep-learning-based approaches for cultural monument analysis and documentation in Nepal. It brings together research on automatic monument detection, transfer learning, and image-based 3D reconstruction. The broader aim is to explore how AI and computer vision can support digital documentation of heritage sites and future cultural heritage applications.

The research also connects with international digital cultural heritage and open-science activities through the SOI Asia DHASH, UNESCO Nepal initiative.

Funding and collaboration: DHASH (SOI Asia, Japan), UNESCO Nepal, and NAST NepalStatus: Ongoing

Research project 02 · UGC-funded

AI-Based Rhesus Macaque Detection and Repellent System

Artificial Intelligence Object Detection Computer Vision Embedded Systems Non-Lethal Deterrence

Project overview

Human–macaque conflict is a recurring concern in Nepal, particularly in areas where macaques interact with people, residential settlements, and agricultural environments. This project investigates an AI-assisted approach to detecting rhesus macaques and supporting a non-lethal deterrent response.

The project integrates deep-learning-based object detection with an electronic ultrasonic repellent prototype. It explores how computer vision and electronic systems can work together to support early detection and future human–macaque conflict mitigation.

Funding agency: UGC Nepal Status: Ongoing
